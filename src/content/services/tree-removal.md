## How a Tree Removal Actually Works

Very few trees around Bowling Green can simply be felled in one piece. Most yards have a house, a fence, a shed, or a service line somewhere inside the drop zone, so the majority of our removals are done in sections. A climber or bucket operator works down the tree from the top, roping limbs and trunk sections to the ground in controlled pieces rather than letting anything free-fall.

Before any of that starts we assess lean, weight distribution, decay, and escape routes, and we identify anything that changes the plan — a hollow trunk, a split union, or a tree already under tension from a partial failure. Storm-damaged trees in particular are often loaded with stored energy, and cutting one in the wrong order is how people get hurt. Once the tree is down we cut the wood to manageable lengths, chip the brush, and haul everything away. If you want the wood left for firewood, say so and we will stack it instead.

## When a Tree Can Be Saved Instead

Not every problem tree needs to come out. Deadwood in an otherwise healthy canopy can be pruned. A tree that has lost a single major limb will often recover with proper cuts. Even some leaning trees are perfectly stable and have been sitting at that angle for years. We would rather tell you a tree is fine than sell you a removal you do not need, so if you are unsure, have someone look at it before you commit to taking it down.

Removal genuinely makes sense when decay has compromised a significant share of the trunk, when major roots are severed or the root plate is heaving, when a structurally weak species is growing close to something it could land on, or when the tree is already dead. Standing dead trees are worth dealing with sooner rather than later — the wood degrades, the tree can no longer be safely climbed, and the job gets more difficult and more expensive the longer it waits.
