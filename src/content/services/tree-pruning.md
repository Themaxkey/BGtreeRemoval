Tree pruning keeps your trees healthy, safe, and attractive year-round. There are many reasons why tree pruning is important: removing branches that are diseased or damaged, clearing overhanging limbs that can obstruct utilities or signage, and thinning branches to improve sunlight and airflow throughout the canopy.

## Why Regular Tree Pruning Matters

Regular pruning also encourages stronger structural growth, reducing the risk of storm damage and costly emergency removals down the road. Our team follows proper pruning techniques to keep your trees healthy for years to come.

## Signs Your Trees Need Pruning

Look out for dead, cracked, or crossing branches, limbs hanging low over your roof, driveway, or power lines, and a canopy that has grown noticeably lopsided or overcrowded. Trees that have not been pruned in several years often show thin, sparse growth near the ends of branches. If a tree is too far gone to save through pruning alone, we also offer tree removal.

## When to Prune Trees in Bowling Green

Timing matters more than most homeowners expect. For the majority of hardwoods around Warren County, late fall through early spring — while the tree is dormant and the leaves are down — is the ideal window. The structure of the tree is easy to read, cuts seal quickly once spring growth begins, and there is far less stress on the tree overall. Dormant pruning also lets us spot weak unions and interior deadwood that a full summer canopy hides completely.

Oaks are the exception worth knowing about. Pruning oaks between roughly April and June raises the risk of oak wilt, a fungal disease spread by beetles that are drawn to fresh cuts. Unless a limb is an immediate hazard, we schedule oak work outside that window. Flowering trees like dogwood and redbud are best pruned just after they finish blooming, so you do not give up next season’s flowers. Storm damage is the one job that never waits — a cracked or hanging limb should come down regardless of the calendar.

## How We Approach a Pruning Job

We start by walking the property and looking at the whole tree, not just the branch you called about. We check for deadwood, crossing or rubbing limbs, weak V-shaped unions, clearance over the roof and driveway, and any growth heading toward the service drop. From there we agree on the scope before anyone leaves the ground.

Cuts are made at the branch collar — never flush to the trunk and never leaving a stub — so the tree can seal the wound properly. We do not top trees. Topping strips a canopy of its structure and forces weak, fast-growing regrowth that tends to fail in the next storm, which creates a bigger hazard than it removes. We also keep removal to a sensible share of the live canopy in any single season so the tree keeps enough leaf area to feed itself. When we finish, limbs and brush are chipped and hauled off and the work area is raked clean.

### What’s the best time of year to prune trees?

Most trees benefit from pruning in late winter or early spring while they’re dormant, though dead or hazardous branches should be removed as soon as you notice them, regardless of the season.

### Will pruning harm my tree?

Not when it’s done correctly. We remove only what’s needed to improve health and structure, using techniques that help the tree heal quickly and keep growing strong.

### How often should trees be pruned?

Most established trees do well with pruning every 2 to 3 years, though young trees and certain species may benefit from more frequent attention.
