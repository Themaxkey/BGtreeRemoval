## From Walkthrough to Cleared Site

Every clearing job starts with a walkthrough of the parcel and a look at your site plan. We need to know where the build envelope ends, which trees are staying, what access looks like for equipment, and whether there are wetlands, easements, or setbacks constraining the work. Marking preserved trees before anyone starts cutting is the single easiest way to avoid an expensive mistake, and it takes ten minutes.

From there the sequence is usually clear and fell, process and remove, then grind stumps as needed for grading. We can haul material off site, chip it on site, or windrow it along a boundary depending on what your project needs and what the site allows.

## Working Around Warren County Ground

The limestone under this region shows up in clearing work more than people expect. Shallow bedrock means stumps sometimes cannot be ground as deep as a grading plan assumes, and karst ground needs to be handled carefully once the stabilizing root structure is gone. Flagging that during clearing lets your grading contractor plan around it, rather than discovering it later with a machine and a change order.

## Erosion Control and Realistic Timelines

Stripping vegetation off a slope changes how water moves across it immediately. We plan the clearing sequence and equipment routes to limit unnecessary disturbance, and we can leave buffer vegetation in place where it makes sense until your erosion controls are in.

On timelines, acreage matters less than density, terrain, and access. A tight two-acre lot full of mature hardwood with one narrow entrance can take longer than five open acres of scrub and cedar. We would rather walk the site and give you a window we can actually hit than quote a number over the phone and revise it later.
