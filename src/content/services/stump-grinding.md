Stump grinding clears the leftover stump after a tree has fallen or been removed, so you can safely reclaim your yard. It grinds the stump and surface roots down below ground level, making the area safe to walk, mow, or landscape over again.

## Why Remove a Tree Stump

Leftover stumps aren’t just an eyesore, they can attract pests, create tripping hazards, and make mowing difficult. Our stump grinding service removes stumps below ground level so you can reclaim your yard and even replant if you’d like.

## What to Expect During Stump Grinding

We start by measuring the stump and checking for nearby utility lines, irrigation, or underground obstacles. Using a grinding wheel, we chip the stump and major surface roots down several inches below grade, then backfill the hole with the ground-up wood chips or soil so the area is level and ready to reseed or landscape.

## Stump Grinding vs. Stump Removal

Stump grinding shreds the stump down below the surface but leaves the root system to decompose naturally underground, while full stump removal digs out the entire root ball. Grinding is faster, less disruptive to the surrounding yard, and usually more affordable, which is why most homeowners choose it after a tree removal. If a stump is infested with pests like the emerald ash borer, grinding also removes the visible habitat and reduces the risk of the infestation spreading.

## What Stump Grinding Actually Involves

Grinding is not the same as digging a stump out. A rotating carbide wheel chips the stump and the major surface roots down into mulch, working several inches below grade rather than excavating the entire root ball. That keeps disruption to your yard small — no machine tearing a trench across the lawn, and no crater left behind to fill.

How deep we go depends on what you plan to do with the space. Six to eight inches below grade is plenty if you are simply re-seeding grass over the spot. If you intend to plant a replacement tree, lay sod, pour concrete, or set fence posts, we grind deeper and clear more of the root mass. It is worth telling us that when you call so we bring the right setup the first time.

## What Happens to the Hole and the Wood Chips

Grinding produces a surprising volume of chips — usually more material than the hole needs, because ground wood takes up more space than the solid stump did. Most customers have us backfill with the chips and mound the spot slightly to allow for settling. Others prefer we haul the chips off and fill with topsoil instead, which is the better choice if you are re-seeding right away, since decomposing wood chips temporarily pull nitrogen out of the soil and can stunt new grass. Either way, expect the area to settle over the following months and need a top-up of soil.

## Locating Utilities Before We Grind

Grinding is a below-grade operation, so buried utilities matter. Before work begins on any stump near a service line, we make sure the site is properly marked. In Kentucky that means an 811 locate request, which is free and required before digging. Keep in mind that a standard locate covers public utilities only — irrigation lines, invisible fence wire, low-voltage landscape lighting, and septic laterals usually are not included, so let us know if you have any of those on the property.
