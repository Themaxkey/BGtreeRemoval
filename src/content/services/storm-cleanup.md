## What to Do Before We Arrive

After a storm, safety comes before cleanup. Treat every downed line as energized — including cable and phone lines, which can become live where they contact a power conductor — and keep people and pets well back from anything a tree is resting on. Do not walk under a limb that is hung up in the canopy. Those are called widow-makers for a reason, and they can release with no warning at all.

If a tree is on your house or a vehicle, photograph it from a safe distance before anything gets moved. Most policies cover removal when a tree has struck a structure, and a claim goes far more smoothly with documentation from before the cleanup started. Then call us and call your insurer.

## Looking at What Is Still Standing

The trees that already came down are only part of the job. After a significant wind event we also look at what survived: fresh cracks in branch unions, root plates lifting on one side, soil heaved up around the base, and canopies that have suddenly gone lopsided. Damage like that often does not fail during the storm that caused it — it fails during the next one. Pointing it out while we are already on site costs you nothing and is a great deal cheaper than an emergency call three weeks later.
