## What Counts as a Tree Emergency

Call immediately if a tree is on your house, garage, or vehicle; if a trunk has split or a root plate has lifted out of the ground; if a large limb is hung up in the canopy above a driveway, walkway, or play area; or if any part of a tree is in contact with a power line. A tree that has visibly shifted since the last storm counts as well — movement means something has already failed, either below ground or inside the trunk, and the tree is no longer holding itself the way it was designed to.

What can usually wait for normal business hours: small branches down in open yard, a tree that has looked the same way for years, and cosmetic damage well away from structures. If you are not sure which category you are in, call and describe it. We would rather talk you through it than have you guess.

## Power Lines Are a Utility Call First

If a tree or limb is touching a power line, that is a call to your electric provider before it is a call to us. No tree service should be cutting on an energized conductor. In the city, Bowling Green Municipal Utilities handles the line side; across much of the rest of Warren County it is Warren RECC. Once the utility has de-energized or cleared the situation, we can safely handle the tree. Until then, treat every downed line as live, keep clear of any standing water near one, and do not attempt to move branches off a line yourself.

## The Cheapest Emergency Is the One That Never Happens

Most of the trees we take down in the middle of the night were showing problems in daylight weeks or months earlier — deadwood through the upper canopy, a seam running down the trunk, fungal conks at the base, or a lean that got slightly worse each season. Having those trees assessed and then pruned or removed on a normal schedule costs a fraction of emergency work, and it does not involve a tarp on your roof or an insurance claim.

If you have a large tree close to the house and you have been wondering about it, have someone look before the next round of spring storms rather than after. An honest assessment is worth having even if the answer is that the tree is fine.
