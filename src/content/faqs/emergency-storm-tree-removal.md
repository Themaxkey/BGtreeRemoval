Yes. We run emergency tree removal around the clock across Bowling Green and Warren County, including nights, weekends, and holidays. Storms here do not keep business hours. Call (270) 721-7671 to reach us about an emergency at any hour.

## What Counts as an Emergency

Call right away if a tree is on your house, garage, or vehicle; if a trunk has split or the root plate has lifted out of the ground; if a large limb is hung up in the canopy over a driveway, walkway, or anywhere people pass; or if any part of a tree is touching a power line.

A tree that has shifted visibly since the storm counts too. Movement means something has already failed, either underground or inside the trunk, and the tree is no longer holding itself the way it was built to.

What can generally wait for normal hours: small branches down in open lawn, debris well away from anything, and a tree that looks exactly as it did last week. If you are unsure which side of the line you are on, call us and describe it — we would rather talk it through than have you guess.

## Before We Get There

Safety comes ahead of cleanup, every time. Keep people and pets well back from anything a tree is resting on, and stay out from under limbs caught up in the canopy. Those hung-up branches are unpredictable and can drop with no warning at all.

Treat every downed line as live, including cable and phone lines, which can become energized where they touch a power conductor. Keep clear of standing water anywhere near a fallen line, and never try to move branches off one yourself.

If a tree has struck your house or car, photograph it from a safe distance before anything gets moved. Most policies cover removal where a tree has hit a structure, and claims go far more smoothly with pictures taken before the cleanup started.

## Power Lines Are a Utility Call First

Where a tree or limb is in contact with a power line, your electric provider needs to be first on the phone. No tree service should be cutting on an energized conductor. In the city that is Bowling Green Municipal Utilities; across much of the rest of the county it is Warren RECC. Once the utility has made the situation safe, we can deal with the tree.

## How We Prioritize When Everything Happens at Once

When a line of storms crosses Warren County we take more calls in an hour than any crew can answer at once, so we triage rather than working strictly in the order calls arrive. Trees on occupied structures come first. Then trees blocking the only access in or out of a property. Then limbs hanging over spaces people use. Debris in open ground is scheduled behind all of that.

If your situation deteriorates while you are waiting — a limb shifts, a crack widens, the tree settles further — call us back and say so. That genuinely moves you up the list.

## What the Work Involves

Storm-damaged trees are not removed the same way healthy ones are. A tree that has partially failed is often loaded with stored tension, and releasing that in the wrong order is how people get hurt. We assess how the weight is sitting, where it will move when cut, and what is underneath before anyone starts.

Where a tree is on a roof, the priority is getting the load off safely without driving damage further into the structure. That usually means dismantling from the top in small roped sections rather than pulling anything.

## Insurance

We are not your insurer and will not tell you what your policy covers, but broadly: cover often applies where a tree has hit a structure, and is less commonly available where a tree simply fell in the yard. Photograph everything first, call your insurer early, and keep documentation of the work. We are glad to provide what you need for a claim.
