The honest answer is that nobody can quote a tree accurately without looking at it. Two oaks of identical height can differ by a thousand dollars depending on what is underneath them. That said, you deserve a ballpark before you pick up the phone, so here is how tree removal pricing actually works and what shapes the number you get.

## Typical Price Ranges by Tree Size

Across the country, most residential tree removals land somewhere between roughly $400 and $1,200, with the average sitting near $850. Broken down by height, small trees under about 30 feet generally run $200 to $500. Medium trees in the 30 to 60 foot range typically fall between $500 and $1,500. Large trees of 60 to 80 feet commonly run $850 to $2,000, and anything over 80 feet frequently starts around $1,200 and climbs from there.

Bowling Green is not a major metro, so local pricing tends to sit at the friendlier end of those national ranges rather than the top. Treat the figures above as a starting frame, not a quote.

## What Actually Drives the Price

Height and trunk diameter. Height gets the attention, but diameter often matters more. Once a trunk passes roughly 24 inches across, the wood volume, cutting time, and equipment demands all rise sharply.

Access. This is the factor homeowners underestimate most. A tree a bucket truck can park beside is a straightforward job. The same tree in a fenced back yard, reachable only through a narrow gate, has to be climbed and roped down piece by piece and carried out by hand. That can double the labor.

What is underneath it. A tree standing in open lawn can be dropped in large sections. A tree leaning over your roof, a neighbor’s fence, or a service line has to be dismantled in small controlled pieces, each one rigged and lowered. More rigging means more hours.

Cleanup scope. Hauling everything away costs more than leaving rounds stacked for firewood. Tell us which you want and it changes the number.

## Why Dead Trees Often Cost More, Not Less

People are frequently surprised by this one. A dead or badly decayed tree is more expensive to remove than a healthy one of the same size, because it cannot be trusted. A climber cannot safely put weight on brittle limbs, decayed wood breaks unpredictably rather than hinging the way sound timber does, and the whole job may require a crane or bucket that a healthy tree would not have needed.

This is also why waiting is a false economy. A dead tree left standing for two more winters is not the same job it was, and it will not be quoted the same way.

## Costs That Are Usually Quoted Separately

Stump grinding is nearly always priced apart from the removal itself, commonly in the $100 to $500 range depending on stump diameter and how deep you need it taken down. Hauling chips off site rather than backfilling with them is another line item. Emergency and after-hours work carries a premium for obvious reasons, and peak storm season generally prices higher than a quiet week in winter simply because demand is higher.

## A Few Things Specific to Bowling Green

Warren County sits on karst limestone, so bedrock is frequently shallow. That mostly affects stump grinding rather than the removal, but it is worth flagging before work starts if you intend to replant or pour over the spot, because we may not be able to grind as deep as you assumed.

Timing matters too. After a line of storms moves through the region, every crew in the county is booked and pricing reflects that. Non-urgent work scheduled in the quieter months is almost always cheaper.
