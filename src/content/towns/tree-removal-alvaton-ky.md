Alvaton lies roughly nine miles southeast of Bowling Green along the US 231 Scottsville Road corridor. It is an unincorporated community in southeastern Warren County, best known geographically for sitting on Drakes Creek, a substantial tributary of the Barren River that is gauged here by the USGS.

## Tree Removal in Alvaton, KY

Drakes Creek is what makes tree work around Alvaton distinctive. Creek bottoms grow a different set of trees from the surrounding uplands — sycamore, river birch, black willow, silver maple and box elder all favour wet ground — and those species behave differently when they fail. They grow fast, they grow large, and their wood is comparatively weak. Silver maple in particular is notorious for shedding limbs in storms.

## Creek Banks and Root Plates

The bigger issue on creek-side property is what the water does to the ground. Bank erosion undermines root plates gradually and invisibly. A tree can look completely healthy in the canopy while the soil that anchors it on the creek side is slowly being carried away, and the first obvious sign is often a lean that appears after a wet spring.

If you have mature trees along Drakes Creek or any of the smaller runs feeding it, the things worth watching are soil cracking or mounding on the upslope side, exposed roots where the bank has cut back, and any change in lean from one season to the next. Those are the trees we would want to look at before a storm rather than after.
