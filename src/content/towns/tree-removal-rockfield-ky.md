Rockfield sits about eight miles southwest of Bowling Green on US 68 and KY 80, the Russellville Road. It is an unincorporated community with a post office dating back to 1866, and it takes its name from the rocky terrain around the original town site — a detail that turns out to matter a great deal for tree work.

## Tree Removal in Rockfield, KY

Southwestern Warren County sits on the karst limestone that runs beneath most of this region, and around Rockfield the rock is close to the surface. Shallow bedrock changes how trees grow and how they fail. Roots cannot go down, so they spread wide and shallow instead, which leaves even large healthy trees poorly anchored against wind once the ground is saturated.

It also changes stump grinding. Where there is rock a few inches down, there is a hard limit on how deep a stump can be taken, and no amount of equipment changes that. If you are planning to replant, lay sod or pour over the area, tell us when you call so we can look at the ground before quoting rather than after.

## The December 2021 Tornado

The EF3 tornado of 11 December 2021 touched down just west of Rockfield in the early hours of the morning, intensifying as it crossed KY 1083 before tracking northeast toward Bowling Green on what became a twenty-nine mile path. It was a serious event for this community.

Storm damage of that scale leaves a long tail. Trees that stayed upright can still carry cracked branch unions, partially lifted root plates and torn attachments, and those defects often do not cause a failure until a much more ordinary storm years later. Any large tree that came through 2021 and has since developed a lean, a seam in the trunk or a thinning canopy is worth having looked at.
